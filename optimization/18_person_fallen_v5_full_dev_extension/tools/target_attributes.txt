VIEW AND IDENTITY RULES
The first image is the complete scene and defines all person identities and positions. If present, the second image is a magnified region of the SAME original image, not a different scene. A crop may contain multiple people. Use it to clarify each person's details, but do not assume one crop equals one person. Count the same person across views only once. IMPORTANT POSTURE BOUNDARIES
- floor_sitting: the pelvis is the primary lower support and the head/shoulders are visibly above the hips. Legs may be straight, bent or crossed. The person may lean backward, use one or both hands for support, or touch a wall. Hands on the floor, extended legs, a non-rest location, leaning against a wall, or pelvis-floor contact do NOT make a seated person lying.
- Pelvis-floor contact is different from a broad area of the torso being supported by the ground. Do not describe a seated person's torso contact as broad merely because their hips or hands touch the floor.
- supine, side_lying, prone and curled_lying describe lying postures: broad torso support by the supporting surface or an approximately horizontal shoulder-to-hip axis. Distinguish ground support from normal bed/sofa/rest support. Ground lying depends on actual human posture and support, not on whether a location seems unsuitable for rest.
- crawling: hands and knees/feet support the body on all fours with the torso off the ground. pushup_plank likewise has hand/foot or forearm/foot support and torso off the floor. These are different from a torso broadly resting on the ground. Do not convert crawling into maintenance merely because equipment is nearby.
- kneeling, squatting, bending, standing and walking must be described for the corresponding person, not confused with a separate ground-lying person's horizontal torso.
- Lying or resting on a bed, sofa or recliner is not lying on the ground. Report the actual support_surface.
- explicit_work_evidence=yes requires visible tools/materials being used, a cleaning/repair action, a dismantled/operated object, or a clear person-to-work-object relationship FOR THIS PERSON. Merely being near equipment, tools in the background, or another person working is insufficient. One person's work must not negate another person's lying posture.
- Use unknown/insufficient when the relevant visual attribute cannot be determined. Do not invent confident posture, support or complete coverage from missing evidence.
